Key Findings
Zero high-priority issues remain. The check that flagged eight problems this morning — titles over 60 characters, descriptions outside 120–160, pages under 300 words, missing H1s, images without alt text — now returns nothing across all 17 pages.

The two thin-content problems are gone. /areas/ went from 207 words to 487, and the four thin town pages from 278–299 to 336–406. Average page length rose from 418 to 486 words, and the shortest page on the site is now 336.

Schema coverage is 15 of 17. The two exceptions are the privacy policy and terms, which do not need structured data. Both hubs now carry CollectionPage + ItemList + BreadcrumbList.

Internal linking now carries signal. Link instances per page range from 25 to 43 across nine distinct values, where every page previously sat at exactly the same count. Service pages sit at the top of that range and the legal pages at the bottom, which is the correct shape.

Security headers are live except CSP, which is omitted on purpose. Images are WebP throughout, with total payload down 49%.

The one content issue still open: recurring and deep cleaning overlap 37.3%, improved from 46.7% but still the highest pair on the site.

Technical

No Content-Security-Policy — deliberate

The other four headers are live: nosniff, strict-origin-when-cross-origin, SAMEORIGIN, and a Permissions-Policy, alongside HSTS and Brotli.

CSP was omitted on purpose. The LeadConnector chat widget and Google Analytics both inject script, and a wrong policy breaks them silently with no console error the customer would ever report. Worth adding only with careful staged testing.
Low

Sitemap lastmod is build date, not content date

All 17 URLs stamp the current date on every deploy, including pages that did not change.

Google discounts lastmod when it is obviously unreliable, so this is harmless. Only worth fixing if you want the field to mean something, which needs real per-page modification tracking.

Internal Linking
The structural weakness from the last audit, now resolved
A correction to the previous report. It said every page had "16 in / 17 out — that's flat, fix it." That metric counted unique link targets, which can never vary on a site whose nav and footer link to every page. It was a misleading way to frame the problem.

The right measure is link instances and anchor text. Those have both changed: instance counts now range from 25 to 43 across nine distinct values, and area pages are reached by descriptive in-sentence anchors rather than only footer labels.
