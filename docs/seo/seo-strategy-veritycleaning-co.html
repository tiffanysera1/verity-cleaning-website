Key Findings
The site is in genuinely good technical shape. Across 17 pages there are no duplicate titles, descriptions, or H1s, no orphan pages, no broken internal or external links, and every page carries a unique canonical, a viewport, complete OpenGraph, and a Twitter card. Image alt text is correct throughout — the decorative images use empty alt deliberately, which is right.

The most consequential finding is internal linking that carries no information. Every one of the 17 pages has exactly 16 inbound and 17 outbound internal links. That perfect uniformity means 100% of link equity flows through the shared nav and footer, and not one link is contextual. Google uses internal linking to infer which pages matter; right now the site tells it that all 17 matter identically — including the privacy policy.

Second: the new area pages are thin. Excluding nav and footer, /areas/ carries 207 words, and Helena (281), Alabaster (278), Homewood (288) and Vestavia Hills (299) all sit under 300. They aren't doorway pages — measured body overlap peaks at 34.7% — but thin pages competing against national franchises will struggle.

Third: the two hub pages have no structured data. /services/ and /areas/ are the highest-value navigational pages on the site and the only content pages emitting no JSON-LD at all.

One old finding is still open: security headers. CSP, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options and Referrer-Policy remain absent — flagged in the previous audit and never addressed. HSTS and Brotli are in place.

Who You're Actually Competing With
Live search results for your core terms, August 2026
The single most useful finding in this audit. Search "house cleaning Pelham AL" and the results are not mostly cleaning companies — they are directories. BBB, Care.com, The Toolbox Pro and a veteran-business directory all rank, alongside two national franchises. Only two genuine local independents appear. That tells you where authority lives in this vertical, and it reframes what "getting backlinks" means for you.
The three competitor types

National franchises — MaidPro, The Maids

Enormous domain authority, dedicated /pelham-al landing pages, decades of accumulated links. MaidPro leads with a "49-Point Checklist"; The Maids with brand recognition.

Do not try to outrank these on generic head terms. Beat them on specificity and proof: real before/after photos of local jobs, named neighborhoods, and a real owner. Franchise pages are templated across hundreds of cities and read that way.
Hard to beat

Directories — BBB, Care.com, The Toolbox Pro, aggregators

These occupy multiple positions on page one. Notably, The Toolbox Pro's Pelham page already references Highway 31, Shelby County 52 and Oak Mountain State Park — the same hyperlocal signal strategy your area pages use.

You cannot outrank a directory, so join it. Every one is a listing you can claim — which is exactly the backlink strategy on the next tab.
Join, don't fight

Local independents — Absolute Cleaning Solutions, Baza Clean, Clean Queens

These are your real competition and they are beatable. Baza Clean uses a county-and-city URL structure similar to your /areas/ approach. Clean Queens markets on Jefferson/Shelby coverage.

Your advantages: genuine before/after photography, five-star reviews with real detail, a named owner who does the work, and structured data most independents do not bother with. Press those.

Backlinks That Actually Matter
Ranked for a one-month-old local service business
Read this first. For a local cleaning company, "backlinks" mostly means citations — consistent listings of your name, address and phone on directories Google already trusts. Classic link building (guest posts, outreach, digital PR) is largely wasted effort at your stage. The evidence is in the SERP itself: directories outrank most actual cleaning companies for your terms. Google trusts them, so a listing on them transfers trust to you.

Every listing must use identical details: Verity Cleaning LLC · 103 Eagle Cove Drive, Pelham, AL 35124 · (205) 946-0304. Inconsistent NAP across directories actively damages local ranking — worse than having fewer listings.
