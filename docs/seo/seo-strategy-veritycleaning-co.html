Key Findings
The site was invisible to crawlers in two basic ways: /robots.txt and /sitemap.xml both returned 404, so Google had no map of the 7 pages and no crawl directives. Both now generate. Separately, every canonical tag and og:url pointed at the bare apex veritycleaning.co, while Vercel serves the site at www. and 308-redirects the apex — so Google was told "the real version is over here," followed it, and got bounced back. That contradictory signal is now resolved onto a single host.

The five service pages carried no structured data at all — only the homepage had it. They now emit Service, BreadcrumbList, and FAQPage markup, the last built from FAQ content that was already on the page but invisible to Google. FAQ rich results are among the easiest local SERP real-estate wins available.

What remains is strategic, not technical. The site has no page for any of the seven towns it serves. Every page says "Shelby County," but people search "house cleaning Pelham AL" and "move out cleaning Alabaster." With a verified Google Business Profile already in place, city pages are the single largest remaining source of qualified organic traffic. The second gap: the five service pages share a near-identical template skeleton (What's Included → What to Expect → Ready for a fresh start?), which limits how distinctly each can rank.

Fixed This Session
Nine issues found and corrected in code — pending deployment
Verification: all changes confirmed by a clean production build (TypeScript passing, 11 routes generated) and by parsing the resulting HTML in out/. Structured data was parsed and validated as JSON; host references were counted across every built page.

sitemap.xml did not exist (404 in production)

Google had no canonical list of the site's pages. For a 7-page site this is survivable but slows discovery of every new page indefinitely.

Added app/sitemap.ts generating all 7 indexable URLs from the same SERVICES array the pages are built from — so a new service can never be missing from the sitemap.
Fixed

robots.txt did not exist (404 in production)

No crawl directives and, more importantly, no sitemap reference — the standard way crawlers discover a sitemap without manual submission.

Added app/robots.ts: allows all crawlers, declares the canonical host, and points to sitemap.xml.
Fixed

Canonical host conflict: apex declared, www served

Every page's canonical and og:url said https://veritycleaning.co/. That URL 308-redirects to https://www.veritycleaning.co/. Google was pointed at a URL that immediately redirects away — a self-contradicting signal that splits ranking signals between two hosts.

Set metadataBase and all structured-data URLs to the www host Vercel actually serves. Verified: 0 bare-apex references remain across the build, 176 www references.
Fixed

Service pages had zero structured data

Only the homepage carried JSON-LD. The five service pages — the ones meant to rank for commercial-intent searches — had none, so Google had no machine-readable statement of what service is offered, by whom, or where.

Added components/ServiceJsonLd.tsx emitting a linked @graph: Service (with provider pointing at the homepage's LocalBusiness @id and all 7 cities as areaServed), plus BreadcrumbList and FAQPage.
Fixed

FAQ content existed on every service page but was unmarked

Each service page already renders 4 real questions and answers via ServiceResources. Without FAQPage markup, Google reads it as ordinary body text and it can't surface as a rich result or in People Also Ask.

The FAQ block is now generated into FAQPage schema directly from the same detail.faq data the page renders — so the markup can never drift from the visible content, which is what Google requires.
Fixed

Homepage title carried no service or location keyword

Was Verity Cleaning — A Clean Home, More Time Back. Brand-only titles work for businesses people already search by name; for a new business with no brand recognition, the title is the strongest on-page relevance signal and it was spending all of it on a tagline.

Now Pelham & Shelby County House Cleaning | Verity Cleaning (55 chars) — service and place first, brand last. The tagline is preserved in the OpenGraph title, where it still does its job on social shares.
Fixed

Services index H1 was "View All Services"

A navigational instruction, not a description of the page's subject. Zero keyword value on a page that should rank for the category term.

Now Cleaning Services in Shelby County, AL.
Fixed

Three titles and all six descriptions exceeded display limits

Titles ran to 71 characters and descriptions to 216 — truncated with an ellipsis in results, cutting off the call to action and wasting the click-through pitch.

All 7 pages now sit within 60-char titles and 160-char descriptions. Service titles switched from "Shelby County" to "Pelham, AL" — the city name is both shorter and the stronger local search term.
Fixed

Service and services-index pages had no og:image

Links shared to Facebook, Nextdoor, or text message rendered without a preview image — a measurable hit to click-through on exactly the channels a local service business relies on.

Each service page now uses its own service photo as the OG image; the services index uses the hero image.

H1 carries no keyword — deliberate tradeoff, your call

The H1 is Get your weekends back. It's a strong hook and clearly a deliberate design choice, but it contains neither "cleaning" nor any place name. The H1 is a meaningful relevance signal and this one spends it entirely on emotion.

I did not change it — rewriting the hero headline is a brand decision, not an SEO cleanup. Instead the keywords went into the subheading directly beneath it ("House cleaning in Pelham, Alabaster, Helena & Hoover"), and the title tag now carries the full term. If you want maximum on-page signal, the H1 itself would need to read something like "House Cleaning in Pelham, AL — Get Your Weekends Back."

Content Reach

No landing page for any of the seven towns served

The schema claims areaServed for Pelham, Helena, Alabaster, Hoover, Columbiana, Chelsea, and Calera. The site has pages for services, and pages for nothing else. There is no URL that can rank for "house cleaning Alabaster AL" because no page is about Alabaster.

Build /areas/[city] pages — but only with genuinely distinct content per town. Templated city pages with the name swapped are doorway pages under Google's spam policies and carry real penalty risk. This is why I did not auto-generate them: seven pages of substantive local content is a writing task, not a code task.
High

No reviews or testimonials anywhere on the site

Correct as things stand — the business has no customers yet, and the project has a standing rule against star-rating or review-count claims. But review signals are among the heaviest local pack factors, and the site currently offers no social proof of any kind.

As real jobs complete, collect Google reviews through the GHL post-booking flow already specced, then surface genuine quoted testimonials on the site. Only add Review/aggregateRating schema once it reflects real, verifiable reviews — fabricated review markup triggers manual actions.
High

No blog or informational content

serviceDetailData.tsx contains a blogLinks field on every service, suggesting a blog was planned. None exists. Informational content ("how often should you deep clean," "move-out cleaning checklist") is how a local site earns links and captures early-funnel searches.

Lower priority than city pages for a business that needs calls now, but the data structure is already there when you want it.
